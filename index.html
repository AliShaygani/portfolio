Enclosure Wall Bracket

Overview:
Designed a sheet-metal wall-mount bracket that holds a 20 kg electrical enclosure 100 mm off the wall, made from a single laser-cut blank with two press-brake bends and no welds.
Responsibilities:
✅ Defined the requirements: 294 N design load per bracket (3× handling factor), FoS ≥ 2 on yield, ≤ 1.0 mm sag.
✅ Compared three concepts with hand calculations and a weighted decision matrix; selected a triangular web.
✅ Modeled the bracket in SolidWorks Sheet Metal and ran FEA in SolidWorks Simulation with a mesh convergence study.
✅ Iterated on thickness, web height and washers after FEA showed the bolted joint, not the arm, was the critical spot.
✅ Produced the manufacturing drawing (BRKT-001) with GD&T per ASME Y14.5-2018.
Tools & Methods:
🛠️ SolidWorks Sheet Metal | SolidWorks Simulation (FEA) | Hand Calculations | GD&T | DFM
Results & Impact:
🔩 Final design in 3 mm steel (0.78 kg): FoS 3.3, 0.40 mm deflection and a buckling factor of 26.7, meeting every strength and stiffness requirement.

Pressure Vessel

Overview:
Static FEA and wall-thickness optimization of a 72 m³ skirt-supported cylindrical pressure vessel with an 88 barg (8.8 MPa) design pressure and SA-516-60N shell and heads, with the goal of reducing weight and cost.
Responsibilities:
✅ Modeled the shell, heads, nozzles and skirt support in SolidWorks from the vessel's engineering drawing.
✅ Set up static structural FEA in ANSYS Workbench: internal design pressure on the inner surfaces, fixed skirt, mesh refined at the shell and heads.
✅ Ran a wall-thickness study at 135, 130, 125 and 124 mm, tracking peak von Mises stress and deformation.
Tools & Methods:
🛠️ SolidWorks (3D Modeling) | ANSYS Workbench (Static Structural) | Thickness Optimization
Results & Impact:
📈 Peak stress sat at the nozzle junctions in every run, rising from 247 MPa at 135 mm to 258 MPa at 124 mm. Thinning the wall from 135 to 125 mm cut vessel weight by 9% (95.6 t → 87.0 t).

Manual Water Pump

Overview:
Designed a lever-operated piston pump for 18.9 L water jugs that fits any standard 55 mm opening, can be 3D printed in PLA, and comes apart for printing and cleaning.
Responsibilities:
✅ Compared screw, air-pressure and piston pump concepts; selected a piston pump with ball check valves.
✅ Modeled the shell, piston, rod, lever and lid in SolidWorks and Fusion 360, designed for FDM printing with minimal supports.
✅ Specified H7/g6 sliding fits for the three pins (0.005–0.029 mm clearance).
✅ Estimated O-ring break-away friction (18.1 N) and lever mechanical advantage (1.29) to size the handle load.
✅ Ran static FEA in ANSYS Workbench on the handle and piston rod, including a 50 N overload case, and a range-of-motion study in Fusion 360.
Tools & Methods:
🛠️ SolidWorks | Fusion 360 | ANSYS Workbench (FEA) | ISO Fits & Tolerances | Design for 3D Printing
Results & Impact:
🌊 37.6 mL per stroke (target 30 mL) with a 14 N handle force. Optimizing the piston rod against PLA's 37 MPa strength with a 1.5 safety factor cut its thickness by 57% (24.1 MPa peak vs 24.6 MPa allowable) and saved about 26 minutes of print time.
